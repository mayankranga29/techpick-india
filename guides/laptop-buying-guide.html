LAPTOP BUYING GUIDE

How to choose a laptop: start with your workload

The best laptop is not necessarily the one with the biggest numbers. Start by listing what you will actually do: browse and study, write code, edit photos or video, play games, or carry out demanding professional work.

1. Choose a processor for your tasks

The processor (CPU) handles general computing instructions. Compare the exact processor model, its generation, power class and independent reviews rather than relying only on a brand name or the number of cores. Two processors with similar names can perform differently depending on their power limits and cooling.

2. Check memory (RAM)

RAM holds the data your open apps are actively using. More memory can help when you keep many browser tabs, development tools and other apps open. Check whether the RAM is soldered, replaceable or upgradeable before buying.

3. Prefer an SSD for everyday responsiveness

An SSD stores your operating system, apps and files. It generally makes booting and loading apps feel much faster than an older mechanical hard drive. Check the capacity and whether the laptop has an accessible slot for future expansion.

4. Look beyond display size

Consider resolution, brightness, colour coverage, panel type and refresh rate. A high refresh rate can make motion look smoother, but brightness and colour quality may matter more for study, office work or creative tasks.

5. Research battery life carefully

Manufacturer battery figures are measured under specific conditions. Look for independent tests that resemble your workload, and remember that screen brightness, performance mode and background apps affect runtime.

6. Check the details people often miss

Review port selection, webcam quality, keyboard comfort, weight, charger size, warranty terms, service availability and upgrade options. These details can affect everyday satisfaction as much as headline specifications.

Before you decide

Compare the exact model number and configuration, read more than one credible review, and verify the final specifications and return policy with the seller. Prices and configurations change, so re-check them on the day you buy.
